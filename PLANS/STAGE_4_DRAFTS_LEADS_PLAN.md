# Plan: Stage 4 — Validation, Local Drafts, Leads, and Safe Notifications

## What and why

Stage 4 turns the straight-kitchen configurator into a complete enquiry flow. A visitor can safely leave and return, review a configuration against the latest published catalog and prices, accept any visible price change, provide the minimum contact details, and receive one reference number even after a double tap or network retry.

The owner receives every stored enquiry in the protected cabinet even when Telegram is unavailable or deliberately disabled. Direct contact data, configuration, and price are kept out of browser drafts, Telegram, logs, and public share links; contact data is encrypted in PostgreSQL and locally stored lead data is removed on the approved retention schedule. If Telegram is enabled, the opaque reference is treated as a pseudonymous identifier rather than declared non-personal, so launch also requires explicit approval of this minimal provider disclosure and its external retention limits. The lead form cannot be enabled in production until the owner has approved the operator details, privacy notice, standalone consent wording, retention period, recovery window, and either a Telegram-disabled policy or the Telegram disclosure.

## Stage 4A acceptance contract

Stage 4A lead capture is ready when all of the following are observable. Full Stage 4 closes only after the separately gated Stage 4B share-link increment also passes its contract.

1. A visitor builds a kitchen on a 360 × 640 viewport, refreshes the page, and restores the current configuration without re-entering it; no company, person, phone, messenger value, or consent flag is present in local storage.
2. A saved draft from an older publication is revalidated by the server. Changed prices are shown as old versus new, unavailable variants stay visible with a replacement/removal action, and submission remains blocked until all issues are resolved and the current price is explicitly accepted.
3. One successful submission transaction stores one immutable configuration/price snapshot, one encrypted contact payload, the exact legal-document versions, and one policy-bound notification row: deliverable when Telegram was approved for that lead, terminally suppressed otherwise. While the lead remains within its approved lifetime, repeating the same completed request returns the original reference even after a later publication; erasure switches to the bounded `410` tombstone contract.
4. The protected owner inbox and detail screen remain usable while Telegram is unavailable or disabled. In enabled mode Telegram receives only an opaque pseudonymous reference, status, and a general protected-inbox link; it receives no direct contact, configuration, or price. Automatic delivery never repeats an outcome that might already have succeeded: an ambiguous timeout becomes `delivery_uncertain` and requires an explicit owner resend with a duplicate warning. In disabled mode no provider call occurs, and later enablement never backfills old suppressed rows.
5. An expired or manually erased lead is removed by a repeatable scheduled task under an explicit owner/legal retention allowlist; no row-level configuration is silently reclassified as anonymous. Backup retention does not exceed the approved recovery window, off-server copies are encrypted before upload, and a restore drill proves that overdue and post-backup erasures run before restored data can serve public traffic.

The primary signal is the phone flow `build → refresh/restore → reconcile a changed publication → review → submit → open the same lead in the owner cabinet`.

## Key decisions

- **D-1. Configuration drafts contain no personal data.** The draft record stores only versioned configuration command, UI-safe options, last confirmed quote summary and timestamps. Contact/consent live only in final-form memory and clear on success/page exit. D-32 uses a separate namespaced, non-exported recovery-handle record containing only random capabilities—no contact, reference, configuration, price or consent—and treats it as sensitive bearer state: no logs/analytics/sync. A committed handle remains until explicit “new enquiry/close result”; abandoned handles clear only after WORM-backed terminal confirmation.
- **D-2. Draft storage is schema-versioned and fail-closed.** `@seriya/contracts` owns the local draft schema. A known old version is migrated explicitly; an invalid or unknown version is quarantined and the visitor sees a clear “could not restore” action instead of silently applying partial data.
- **D-3. Undo/redo is a bounded configuration history.** Every meaningful configuration mutation goes through one command boundary. The client keeps up to 50 snapshots in memory, clears redo after a new action, and does not treat selection, open sheets, quote responses, contact input, or consent as undoable product state.
- **D-4. Autosave persists the current configuration, not the undo log.** A debounced save follows successful local mutations, while the latest validated no-contact serialization is synchronously flushed on `visibilitychange` to hidden, `pagehide`, and explicit in-app navigation before any privacy shield or unload completes. A `storage` event warns about another tab rather than merging competing anonymous drafts.
- **D-5. Restore and pre-submit reconciliation are server-owned and disclose only publications that were actually public.** A new configuration reconciliation operation recalculates the saved command against its immutable original publication and the active publication. The original is eligible only when its durable `publicVerifiedAt` proves a successful public activation; the current snapshot is eligible only when `CatalogState.activationState=IDLE`, its pointer/status/checksum agree, and it also has `publicVerifiedAt`. Candidate, prepared, failed, rolled-back, ambiguous legacy, and maintenance publications fail closed without returning snapshot contents. A one-time application command (not handwritten migration SQL) marks only publications proven by a verified activation attempt plus the currently active Stage 3 invariant; ambiguous history stays ineligible. The browser never supplies a trusted old price, availability result, material rate, or difference list.
- **D-6. Unavailable selections remain visible until the visitor acts.** Reconciliation returns stable item-level issues and replacement context. An exact new total is returned only when the active publication can price the full configuration; otherwise submission remains blocked.
- **D-7. Price acceptance is explicit and narrow.** The visitor accepts a specific active `publicationId`, `priceVersionId`, normalized configuration fingerprint, and total. Any subsequent configuration mutation or publication conflict clears that acceptance.
- **D-8. Submission recalculation and lead creation use one explicit unit of work and one PostgreSQL transaction.** The leads application calls a `LeadSubmissionUnitOfWork` port rather than the existing request-oriented `ConfigurationHttpService`. Its composition-root infrastructure adapter alone owns the Prisma transaction: it follows the full D-39 lock order, locks and rechecks the active policy plus current `LeadPolicyTransitionState`/operation revision, phase, effective cap, Telegram decision and capture block, loads the active immutable publication/pricing snapshot through transaction-bound repository adapters, passes those already-loaded values to the configuration module's exported pure calculator, validates the accepted publication/price/fingerprint, and writes the lead, exactly one policy-bound notification row, and submission-key registry commit with that same transaction client. `telegram_enabled` always creates `pending`; `telegram_disabled` always creates terminal `suppressed`. No Prisma type crosses into application/domain code, and leads imports neither configuration nor catalog/pricing infrastructure internals. The transaction cannot call a service backed by the root Prisma client, perform an unlocked pre-quote, or trust a browser total/specification.
- **D-9. One globally unique submission-key registry owns idempotency for a bounded, explicit lifecycle.** The browser creates one submission key as the literal `sk_` prefix plus a 43-character unpadded base64url encoding of 256 CSPRNG bits, persists its separate no-contact recovery capability, and reuses the submission key until committed success or server-confirmed abandonment. `SubmissionKey` and `LeadRecoveryCapability` are separate branded contract types with distinct literal prefixes and HMAC domains; their decoders require exactly 46 characters, preserve all 256 random bits, and reject padding, alternate encodings, wrong prefix/length, non-canonical alphabet and cross-use. The sole live PostgreSQL product-registry digest moves `open → submitting → committed → tombstoned`, or `open|submitting → abandonment_pending → abandoned_tombstone`; no other live product table copies it. D-31/D-48's encrypted, access-isolated recovery markers are the deliberate exception needed to reconstruct a post-backup tombstone, and never become a second query authority. `openExpiresAt` is 24 hours; submit claim has a random token/lease. Claim, expiry, abandon, stale-claim recovery, commit, and erasure serialize on this row. Expiry/explicit abandon may fence an unclaimed `open`; stale-claim recovery uses D-48's direct CAS from the exact expired `submitting` claim and never reopens it. Submit/claim/commit reject the pending fence. Pending is never rolled back on an ambiguous external result: coordinator retries/forward-reconciles until marker+checkpoint, then CAS-terminalizes. `POST /api/leads` never creates a missing row. Abandoned tombstones deny reuse for 30 days, then allow cleanup/version retirement. The envelope is `{ idempotencyKey, submissionSchemaVersion, receiptToken, payload }`; lookup precedes current validation. Commit stores fingerprint/lead relation and a separate long-lived D-32 recovery capability hash. Same-content replay returns original reference; altered payload/schema is `409`; erasure clears relation/fingerprint, becomes `tombstoned`, and returns `410`.
- **D-10. Lead creation and its policy-bound notification decision are atomic.** One database transaction creates the lead and exactly one notification row. The active policy's immutable delivery mode and, when enabled, exact Telegram delivery-config version/fingerprint are copied onto the lead/notification: `telegram_enabled` creates `pending`, while `telegram_disabled` creates terminal `suppressed`. Telegram delivery is never part of the request transaction and cannot roll back or lose an accepted lead. Later policy enablement affects only new leads; suppressed historical rows are never backfilled or manually resendable.
- **D-11. Public lead references are unique 128-bit random values.** Each reference is generated from 128 CSPRNG bits and encoded as unpadded URL-safe base64, has a database unique index, and reveals no lead volume. A conflict on that exact constraint rolls back the whole D-8 transaction and the outer unit-of-work retry starts a fresh transaction/reference; at most five attempts run, after which stable `503 REFERENCE_GENERATION_FAILED` is returned with no partial commit. Other constraint errors never retry. The success view and Telegram may display the reference, but it never appears in a browser-history or API request URL; internal UUIDs are never exposed. Deterministic collision tests cover retry and exhaustion.
- **D-12. The contact form is deliberately minimal.** Required values are company name, contact name, phone, preferred messenger (`telegram`, `whatsapp`, or `max`), and one messenger contact. There is no free-text comment field in Stage 4, preventing accidental storage of unrelated personal or sensitive data.
- **D-13. Contact data is encrypted at the application boundary with recoverable versioned keyrings and codecs.** PostgreSQL stores authenticated AES-256-GCM contact payload with schema/key version, 96-bit nonce/tag, and AAD bound to DB lead ID/version. Registries retain every supported submission/contact/snapshot/recovery/control/fingerprint/share version. Contact encryption plus fingerprint, idempotency, recovery-capability, erasure-request, erasure-ledger, notification-config HMAC, recovery-chain signing and distinct D-54 provider-fence phase-signing keyrings retain all unretired versions. Raw idempotency and D-32 recovery tokens are domain-separated HMACed under every unretired key and queried only against `LeadSubmissionKey`; zero/one/multiple version matches mean new/authoritative/integrity failure. New writes use active versions. Runtime private keyrings live only in scoped server config; encrypted offline copy/public verification material lives off-VPS. D-34 prevents retirement while any registry/payload/config/record/marker/attempt/artifact/off-host phase can require a version. Drills cover two rotations and every version.
- **D-14. Legal documents and retention policy use a two-phase candidate/attestation/activation flow.** Candidate creation imports the owner-approved canonical bundle, checksums, operator/retention data and optional Telegram config into immutable inactive state, but returns success/pins config only after a D-46 `candidate_created` record plus D-49 checkpoint; restore can recreate a candidate absent from an older core. A never-active candidate may later receive one WORM-backed withdrawal. `LeadPolicyState` is the only active pointer; lead protection records the current ready pair/coverage/revision and attestation binds a policy checksum to it. First install creates empty artifacts. `policy-transition:start` first establishes a conservative prepared DB fence, then acknowledges D-46 `transition_started` with full candidate, starting revisions, cap, capture block, Telegram decision and irreversibility flags before any purge/cancellation or success response; restore recreates the current transition before readiness. Shorter-window work uses that persistent cap, D-36 adoption and D-38 purge. Activation/abort/withdrawal likewise require D-46+D-49 before terminal DB result. Stale mismatch is `409`; retries return exact command results; submission requires active attestation and stores exact policy/config acceptance.
- **D-15. Consent is separate, affirmative, and bound to canonical document bytes.** Policy documents are canonical UTF-8 plain text with normalized LF line endings and are rendered as text, never executable HTML. The server computes SHA-256 checksums over canonical bytes. `GET /policy` is `no-store` and returns exact text, version, revision, and checksum; the final screen links both documents and uses an unchecked, standalone consent control. A preselected checkbox, consent hidden inside a general submit label, or acceptance inferred from button use is invalid. If submission returns stable `409 POLICY_CHANGED`, the browser keeps contact only in memory, fetches and visibly presents the new exact document, clears the consent control, and requires fresh acceptance. The backend verifies active version/revision/checksum rather than trusting the browser label.
- **D-16. Production lead capture is gated independently from optional sharing.** `LEAD_CAPTURE_ENABLED=false` is the default. Enabling—and every later lead readiness/submission check—requires valid recoverable keyrings/codecs, explicitly including every unretired D-54 phase-signing private/public version in the encrypted offline recovery bundle and its verified public/runtime subset; an active approved policy whose window covers actual lead artifacts; ready lead protection plus fresh attestation; verified privacy logging; current signed/adopted core+lead heads; a verified D-49 `core_lead` projection reaching every completed global/core/lead chain operation while preserving any quarantined share tuple exactly; and an exact checksum match to owner/legal-approved data, primary WORM and independent anchor providers/regions/retention/processor configuration. Any relevant inventory/anchor/provider/keyring mismatch blocks capture. A lead artifact cannot outlive the strictest live-lead/policy/transition window. Retention-only transition may keep capture under its cap; Telegram-affecting transition blocks first submission through activation/safe abort. Stage 4B has separate protection/inventory/provider/readiness, so a share-only defect disables shares only. Enabled Telegram additionally requires approved disclosure/exact config; historical rows retain it or are safely cancelled. Disabled policy creates suppressed rows and activates only after D-40 resolves old work.
- **D-17. Recommended lead retention is 180 days, pending owner/legal approval; erasure is allowlist-based.** Public reference, timestamp, status and snapshot remain potentially personal. At expiry, cleanup completes D-31, then deletes lead/contact/fingerprint/intent/notification/status/snapshot. PostgreSQL retains only bounded tombstoned `LeadSubmissionKey` digests—including D-32 recovery-capability digest needed to return `410`—plus detached proof inventory; encrypted external markers/control records remain only for recovery duty and purge when artifacts/replay semantics permit. They are not analytics. Any aggregate needs separate legal basis/schema. Recommended recovery is 30 days and never exceeds approved personal-data recovery.
- **D-18. The entire owner lead surface is sensitive and leaves no reusable browser state.** Reference, creation time, total, workflow state, and notification state remain pseudonymous/personal even without decrypted contact. Every owner lead list/detail/mutation response therefore uses `Cache-Control: private, no-store`; all owner lead queries live under the session query-key namespace, are never persisted, use `gcTime: 0`, consume cancellation signals/session epoch, and are cancelled and removed on unmount, logout, session expiry, auth bootstrap failure, and cross-tab logout. Detail selection is ephemeral in memory behind a fixed non-reference-bearing owner route; reference/contact values never enter browser-history URLs, query keys, router state, or error text. A page-lifecycle guard clears the entire owner-leads namespace plus forms and renders a privacy shield on `pagehide`; `pageshow.persisted` revalidates the session and refetches before unshielding. Reload/back after a detail view returns to a freshly authorized inbox, not the prior lead.
- **D-19. Lead workflow stays small.** The owner can mark a lead `new`, `in_progress`, or `closed`. Status changes use optimistic revision checks and append a small audit record with actor and timestamp; the immutable submission snapshot is never edited.
- **D-20. Telegram receives only an approved minimal pseudonymous disclosure.** The message contains the random lead reference, a generic status such as “new enquiry”, and a link to the authenticated cabinet root `/app/leads`, never a reference-bearing detail URL, direct contact, configuration, or price. The reference is still treated as a potentially personal pseudonymous identifier. The privacy notice/provider decision must cover Telegram processing, provider retention, and the fact that an accepted or ambiguous message cannot be reliably recalled. Bot tokens and destination chat IDs exist only in versioned server-secret records. If disclosure is not approved, the active policy uses `telegram_disabled`; its terminally suppressed rows never become deliverable after a later policy change, while PostgreSQL and the owner inbox continue to work.
- **D-21. Notification delivery is durable but honest about process and provider boundaries.** A short systemd timer invokes a `leads:notifications:deliver` cron task. It ignores terminal `suppressed` and `cancelled_policy` rows. Claiming locks/checks that an enabled-mode owning lead is `active`, loads only the notification's exact delivery-config version/fingerprint, and writes a `leased` row; there is no fallback to the current environment destination. Immediately before any payload is built or network call begins, a fenced compare-and-set rechecks lifecycle, copied mode/config, and resend authorization and writes `dispatch_started` with token/time. An expired `leased` row without a dispatch marker is safe to reclaim; an expired `dispatch_started` row becomes `delivery_uncertain` and is never auto-sent. Telegram has no idempotency key for `sendMessage`, so only an authoritative `ok=false` response classified as retryable may schedule automatic backoff. A timeout, connection loss after dispatch, malformed response, crash after dispatch start, or other outcome that may have succeeded becomes `delivery_uncertain`. Manual resend additionally requires D-40 authorization for that exact historical config; otherwise it remains visible but blocked. Definitive permanent failure is separately visible.
- **D-22. No new queue or application dependency is introduced.** PostgreSQL is the durable outbox. Telegram delivery uses the official HTTPS Bot API through `fetch`; tests inject a fake transport and never call Telegram.
- **D-23. Every expensive public input has an abuse boundary before parsing/work, and every accepted intent/lead keeps a durable global cleanup guarantee.** `/api/leads/intents`, `/api/leads`, `/api/leads/receipt`, `/api/configurator/reconcile`, current `/api/configurator/quote`, `/api/configurator/replacement-proposal`, and later share create/resolve/revoke receive endpoint-appropriate strict body limits and conservative per-IP in-memory rate limits using the existing trusted-proxy rules before JSON parsing or pricing/database work. Intent creation atomically creates a D-53 reservation and consumes a slot from `LeadAdmissionBudgetState`; fixed UTC-window allowance and hard caps are sized below approved PostgreSQL/WORM/anchor capacity and persist across restart/cutover. Admission proves worst-case cleanup through conservation invariants: `liveTombstones + pendingTombstoneReservations + reservedOpenTombstoneUnits + reservedLeadErasureTombstoneUnits + artifactPinnedTombstoneUnits ≤ tombstoneCap`, `pendingAttempts + reservedOpenAttemptUnits + reservedLeadErasureAttemptUnits + artifactPinnedAttemptUnits ≤ attemptCap`, and `uncheckpointedBatches + uncheckpointedErasureOperations + pendingUnbatchedCleanupReservations + pendingErasureCleanupReservations + reservedOpenCleanupUnits + reservedLeadErasureCleanupUnits + artifactPinnedCleanupUnits ≤ checkpointCap`. One new intent reserves one unit in every open term before its row exists. D-8 commit atomically moves all three open units to their lead-erasure-reserved counterparts, so an accepted lead can always complete mandatory D-31 even after all older artifacts are purged. `open|submitting → abandonment_pending` converts open units to explicit D-48 pending counters without changing totals. Atomic membership of `N` attempts converts `N pendingUnbatchedCleanupReservations → 1 uncheckpointedBatch`, releasing only `N−1`; the last unit releases after D-49 mirror acknowledgement. D-31's first fence transaction converts one lead-erasure reservation into pending tombstone/attempt/erasure-cleanup counters before any external work; attempt preparation moves its cleanup unit to `uncheckpointedErasureOperations`. Terminalization converts `pendingTombstoneReservations → liveTombstones`; attempt and cleanup units move to `free` only when D-53 proves that no selectable artifact can restore their earlier obligation, otherwise they move once to their dimension's artifact-pinned counter. Current duty and an older artifact image of the same reservation are normalized to one unit per dimension, never double-counted; a shared D-48 batch counts once by batch operation ID and a singleton D-31 erasure counts once by erasure operation ID. Tombstone expiry likewise waits for its artifact/marker recovery duty. A full budget fails closed with `429`/`Retry-After` (or `503` for unhealthy recovery), creates no registry/external work, and emits identifier-free capacity status. Restart cannot reset counters. Restore reconstructs D-53 pins and these counters before D-31/D-48 replay; older snapshots saturate/fail closed rather than grant capacity. Batching may coalesce D-48 units but never supplies capacity that an admitted intent or committed lead requires. Lead submission also uses a honeypot; IP addresses remain ephemeral and unlogged. CAPTCHA is deferred until evidence justifies its privacy/usability cost.
- **D-24. Every normal and error sink for sensitive routes is identifier-free.** Application logs and uncaught-error serialization for lead/share flows contain only route templates, allowlisted outcome codes, attempt numbers, and timing—never IP address, public reference, raw URI/query/body, contact input, consent text, full configuration JSON, share/revocation token, bot/chat identifiers, encryption material, or decrypted payloads. All owner lead operations use fixed paths and put references/cursors only in `no-store` request bodies. Provider adapters convert transport failures into fixed internal codes and never serialize the Telegram request URL, headers, body, raw exception, or response. For fixed public/owner lead/share locations, Nginx disables both access logging and request-bearing error logging (or routes them to an equivalent verified sink that cannot emit IP/URI/identifiers); application stdout/stderr and the systemd journal use the same allowlist. Remaining operational logs have a bounded approved rotation. Automated log-capture tests inject sentinels through success, validation, 404, upstream timeout, 5xx, and provider-error paths and assert every forbidden value is absent from application, Nginx access/error, and systemd outputs.
- **D-25. Backup encryption, authenticity, and approved provider placement are launch requirements.** Local backup directories are `0700` and files `0600`. Every data-bearing dump/key bundle is `age`-encrypted before upload; only non-secret signed manifests/heads/checkpoints may remain plaintext. The private recovery key stays off-VPS; server keeps only public recipient and scoped signing material. The encrypted recovery bundle contains every unretired D-54 phase-signing private/public key version needed to continue a nonterminal fence after total VPS loss; manifests bind its version inventory/public-material checksum without exposing private bytes. Data-artifact, primary WORM, and independent D-49 anchor providers/buckets/regions, credentials/administrators, processor/contract basis, localization/cross-border decision, disclosure, lifecycle and immutable retention require owner/legal approval; manifests bind their canonical config checksum. Any mismatch or upload/head/checkpoint/keyring/rotation failure makes readiness red.
- **D-26. Stage 4 adds host tools, not application libraries.** Production installs `age`, a version-compatible PostgreSQL 18 client (`pg_dump`, `pg_restore`, `psql`), and the distro `util-linux` `flock`/`setpriv`. Bun matching `.bun-version` moves from `/root` to a root-owned, world-readable but non-writable versioned runtime under `/opt/seriya/runtime`; service users never traverse `/root`. Only `seriya-backup` connects through localhost with the D-44 read-only role for dumps, and no service uses the Docker socket or root-equivalent `docker` group. `age` remains external and lets backup encrypt without the private recovery key; Ed25519 uses platform crypto. Owner approval of this plan also approves installing these pinned tools during Stage 4 setup.
- **D-27. Restore is a two-pass, fail-before-effect process gated by actual recovered inventory and one audited loader.** The owner decrypts independently encrypted core/scope artifacts and the keyring bundle on a trusted workstation; private recovery material is never copied permanently to the VPS. After signatures/heads/dependencies/checksums are verified, plaintext dump streams pass only through an authenticated ephemeral channel into D-43's one-shot restore loader via inherited file descriptor—never an on-disk release/staging path. Under D-35 exclusive maintenance, a root DB-admin wrapper creates an isolated staging target with `CREATE DATABASE <target> WITH TEMPLATE template0 OWNER seriya_owner`, immediately `REVOKE CONNECT ... FROM PUBLIC`, grants `CONNECT` only to the ephemeral loader login, and passes no admin credential to the child. The loader connects only to that staging target, executes validated startup `SET ROLE seriya_owner`, and runs `pg_restore --no-owner --role=seriya_owner` for core then compatible lead/share streams. Pass 1 is read-only: authenticate every D-46/D-31/D-48 record and scope-separated D-54 phase, verify the highest D-49 global checkpoint and build pure `core_lead` plus optional `share` projected inventories, both digest/version pairs, unique reservation/cleanup IDs, FK closure, version support and D-23 caps. Any global/core/lead failure destroys/quarantines the isolated target and performs zero mutation, swap, provider reconciliation or business effect. A share-only failure is recorded against the exact frozen share tuple and is quarantinable only after D-42 proves its physical tables and re-verifies through the provider that the actual delivered capability identity/effective policy cannot touch global/core/lead; a requested prefix is insufficient. Pass 2 materializes the validated `core_lead` projection inside staging, replays D-46/D-31/D-48, applies cleanup, reconciles relevant D-54, quarantines notifications, rebuilds protection and runs D-45 auth sanitation; it materializes share only if the share projection is independently green. The loader remains `NOCREATEDB`; the wrapper swaps only after core+lead pass 2 is green. Loader membership, target `CONNECT`, credential and plaintext handles are then revoked/removed. A retained release supplies every unretired codec. The signed snapshot-group manifest binds separate artifacts, one exported snapshot, release, dependencies, provider config, and recovery-bundle checksums. Missing/corrupt/quarantined shares keep only shares red and start no share unit.
- **D-28. Anonymous share links are a separate Stage 4B increment with token-safe creation, revocation, and recovery.** Initial lead launch does not depend on sharing. When enabled later, a shared record contains an explicit version of the no-contact draft schema, uses a 256-bit random bearer token whose SHA-256 hash alone is stored, and receives a separate 256-bit creator revocation secret whose SHA-256 hash alone is stored. Create returns the share URL and revocation secret once with `Cache-Control: no-store`; the creator browser stores that capability in a namespaced no-contact local record outside the shared URL, warns that clearing this device loses early-revoke ability, and clears it after revoke or a terminal expired/not-found response. The share expires after 30 days by default, opens as an editable local copy, and is reconciled before quoting or submission. The browser-facing link carries its bearer token only in the URL fragment; fragments do not reach the server. Client code removes the fragment fail-closed with `history.replaceState` before any resolve request or third-party load and sends the token in a `no-store` POST body to a fixed path. Resolve/revoke responses are also `no-store`. Revocation durably records a domain-separated HMAC of the shared-record ID plus ledger key version in the encrypted off-server recovery-deletion register through the same pre-persisted object-key/checksum protocol as D-31 before deleting/marking the row, so an old backup cannot resurrect access; retry after deletion uses privacy-preserving absent-as-success semantics and reveals no prior existence. Scheduled cleanup removes expired/revoked server rows. The share surface sets `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, loads no third-party analytics/resources, and both application/nginx logs omit identifiers, raw URI, and request body.
- **D-29. Stage 4 does not add customer accounts.** Anonymous local drafts, optional later share links, and idempotent leads are enough for the first release; customer sign-in and cross-device saved projects remain deferred.
- **D-30. All schema changes are additive, but rollback has a data-aware floor.** Prisma models and nullable fields are added through the repository migration workflow. Initial rollout is two-phase: deploy Stage 4 with capture disabled, validate policy/keys/backups/inbox/workers, then enable capture separately. Before the first accepted lead, Stage 3 remains an application rollback target. After the first accepted lead, the oldest allowed rollback is a validated Stage 4 maintenance-capable release that retains owner read access, cleanup, decryption, and outbox processing; an incident disables new capture but must not strand existing personal data on Stage 3 code.
- **D-31. Manual erasure and scheduled expiry share one fenced, durable, byte-verifiable recovery-deletion path.** The first transaction creates/reuses the request, sets `erasure_pending`, blocks status/notification, quiesces pre-dispatch work, and atomically moves the D-53 reservation from all three `active_lead_reserved` states to pending tombstone/attempt/erasure-cleanup counters before any external work. Without row locks, the coordinator renders/encrypts marker and computes bytes; a second ordered transaction persists a random-locator prepared attempt and moves `pending_erasure_cleanup → uncheckpointed_erasure(operationId)` before PUT. Exact returned object version is downloaded/rehashed; mismatch fails closed/purges. It appends signed coverage, obtains DB and D-49 acknowledgements, then the final transaction detaches attempts, creates receipt, converts pending tombstone to live tombstone, releases or artifact-pins attempt/cleanup units, clears the registry relation, and deletes the product graph. Encrypted marker contains deletion time/kind, versioned operation digest, keyed lead ID, authoritative idempotency digest/version, authoritative D-32 recovery-capability digest/version, immutable D-53 admission-reservation ID plus its normalized tombstone/attempt/cleanup transitions and replay obligation, and `tombstoneExpiresAt`—never raw capabilities/reference/contact/payload. Restore can recreate the reservation accounting and both idempotency and recovery-capability `410` even if the selected artifact predates the lead. Successful attempts remain until affected artifacts expire/compaction; orphans purge with receipts. Coverage compaction uses D-36 atomic adoption. No required key/version retires early.
- **D-32. Stable success uses a separate long-lived no-contact recovery capability.** Before contact input, final review independently generates `sk_` and `rc_` capabilities, each carrying a full 256-bit CSPRNG payload as 43 unpadded base64url characters after its literal prefix and validated by its own exact 46-character branded contract codec, stores both in D-1's separate recovery-handle record, and creates `LeadSubmissionKey(open, openExpiresAt, recoveryTokenHash)` plus intent. The recovery-token hash lives solely on that row in the live PostgreSQL product graph; only encrypted D-31/D-48 recovery markers carry it for tombstone reconstruction. For uncommitted state, pending visibility ends after 24 hours; registry still follows D-9/D-48. On commit, recovery digest remains for the lead replay lifetime. Rate-limited `no-store` `/receipt` accepts only the `rc_` brand and returns original reference/creation time for `committed` regardless of current policy/publication and without contact/payload; after erasure `410`, after abandonment rotate permission. Browser keeps the handle through reload/tab closure/window expiry until explicit close/new enquiry, while storing no contact/reference. Clearing is local only. Tokens are independently generated, domain-separated, absent from URLs/logs, and authorize no owner data.
- **D-33. Browser history cannot resurrect public contact fields.** The public final form clears contact values and consent synchronously and replaces itself with a blank privacy shield on `pagehide`. Before any bytes are signed, one D-52-ordered transaction holds the D-34 shared selector/version locks and creates `ActiveHostEpochTransition(signing_prepared)` with exact source/target identities, host-chain and checkpoint parents/sequences, selected recovery-chain and D-49 signing-key versions, expected CAS values and null byte/fence links. The authorized signer then creates the exact canonical host/checkpoint bytes, and root fsyncs their checksums in journal phase `signed_prepared`. Both operation IDs, requested selectors/policies, actual capability identities/policy attestations and phase heads are fsynced in the cutover journal. Journal phases are `clean|preparing|signing_prepared|signed_prepared|db_prepared|provider_fences_issued|roles_revoked|host_published|host_db_acked|checkpoint_published|checkpoint_db_acked|provider_fences_terminal|transition_terminal|credentials_revoked`; each transition is temp-rename/file+directory-fsynced. D-34 retirement scans every nonterminal transition pin; a missing/corrupt journal or disagreement with that DB row blocks retirement and cutover. The fixed finalizer and separate D-54 signer advance both operations through read-back and their signed phase chains. The host-record operation requires the host-chain DB CAS and target D-49 checkpoint acknowledgement; like every D-49 write, the checkpoint-record operation uses that exact checkpoint's own read-back plus mirror DB CAS as its non-recursive owning acknowledgement and never creates a checkpoint to acknowledge itself. Both capabilities must then have provider-verified revoke/expiry acknowledgements and matching DB/off-host `completed|drained` terminal phases; `transition_terminal`, finalizer-credential revocation and `clean` are forbidden earlier. If a terminally drained attempt did not publish its required object, recovery prepares a new separately fenced operation for the same conditional key before retrying. Both boot gates block ordinary units on any nonterminal/mismatch; a lead-only release explicitly starts no share unit. Recovery reacquires exclusive and resumes byte-identically. Release occurs only after `host PUT/read-back → host DB CAS → checkpoint PUT/read-back → checkpoint mirror DB CAS → both D-54 fences terminal → transition terminal CAS → finalizer credential revoke → journal clean`; no old/new lead unit starts earlier, and share starts only after its quarantined operations independently reach terminal and its projection is green.
- **D-52. Row creation and FK changes follow the schema graph, not the existing-row pre-lock list.** Each multi-row use case first prelocks every already-existing row it may mutate and every non-mutating existing FK target using D-39; it then creates new rows in a checked parent-before-child topological plan, with same-type IDs stable, and performs an FK-changing update only after both source and target rows are already created/prelocked. PostgreSQL's implicit `KEY SHARE` on every FK target is therefore acquired only against an already locked/created parent and is included in SQL lock traces. Required existing-operation matrices are: first pricing bootstrap creates nullable-empty `PricingState` when absent, then `PriceVersion → PriceDraft`, then points the state; pricing publish prelocks `CatalogState → PricingState → PriceDraft`, creates `PriceVersion`, then updates the two children; catalog import prelocks `CatalogState` plus existing assets/families/source records/variants and creates `CatalogAsset/CatalogSourceRecord → CatalogFamily when its asset exists → CatalogVariant → CatalogImportHistory`; catalog edit prelocks `CatalogState → CatalogVariant` plus any target `CatalogAsset` and actor `User`, updates the variant, then creates `CatalogEditHistory`; publication/activation creates `CatalogPublication` only after its `PriceVersion`, creates `CatalogActivationAttempt` only after both publication parents, and changes the `CatalogState` pointer only after source/target prelocks; recovery uses the same order in reverse-state operations without reverse locking. Schema/FK changes fail architecture validation until every affected matrix and SQL-trace test is updated.

If a current state has advanced beyond an older selectable artifact, the dimension becomes `artifact_pinned` instead of `free`; it is never charged once as current and again as artifact. D-31/D-48 marker outcomes then advance that normalized state exactly once. D-38 denial/purge reconciliation releases an artifact-pinned unit only after primary heads, admitted-artifact inventories and current DB/marker inventory prove no selectable artifact can restore it; live tombstone/marker duties impose the stricter horizon. Restore authenticates and unions every selectable lead-artifact inventory before loading budget state, recreates missing reservation parents before registry/tombstone rows, applies later D-31/D-48 outcomes, recomputes all three inequalities, and fails closed before any replay side effect on missing, duplicate, illegal transition, unknown cleanup operation, checksum drift or cap overflow. Therefore no capacity unit can be reused across backup generations until every older image capable of restoring its obligation is unrecoverable, without adding one WORM write per public intent.
- **D-54. Losing the D-51 database session cannot leave an unfenced late object write.** Before root asks the provider to mint any network capability, one ordered transaction creates `RecoveryProviderOperationFence|LeadRecoveryProviderOperationFence|ShareRecoveryProviderOperationFence(prepared)` in the physical table matching `global|core`, `lead`, or `share`, with operation/host epoch, provider/config and requested allowed-key-prefix checksums, exact deterministic object key and request checksum, intended capability kind, deterministic non-secret issuance/revocation selector, requested normalized IAM/resource policy, requested issue/expiry times, provider-guaranteed maximum accepted-request lifetime, expected parent/sequence and lifecycle revision. Root then conditionally writes and reads back a signed immutable `prepared` phase record under a deterministic scope/host-epoch/operation prefix in a strongly consistent provider-fence control namespace held in the independently administered anchor-store account; its credential is separate from the child and available to disaster/cutover recovery on another host. Only then may the issuer mint the exact-key presigned request or temporary credential while withholding it from the child. The signed/read-back `issued` phase and matching DB CAS add its actual non-secret capability identity, normalized effective IAM/resource-policy hash, provider-authenticated issuance-attestation checksum and actual issue/expiry; the issuer must independently verify those effective rights and may not infer them from the requested policy. Only this completed `issued` mirror authorizes delivery of the single capability to the child. If the issuer crashes between provider issuance and `issued`, recovery discovers the deterministic selector through the prepared phase and revokes/introspects it before retry or drain; the capability was never delivered to a child. An exact-key conditional presigned request must expose no parent credential; otherwise the provider must return an independently verifiable receipt for the actual temporary identity and effective resource policy. Every phase, including `issued`, result, fence request/acknowledgement, each sweep and terminal state, is canonical domain-separated Ed25519-signed bytes containing operation ID, scope, source host epoch, lifecycle revision, previous-phase hash, phase-signing key version and all request/capability-policy/provider receipt/inventory fields. It is conditionally created as the sole child of the preceding hash and read back; the DB mirror stores the exact bytes hash/signature. Strong prefix listing plus signature/parent verification makes the full nonterminal set discoverable and rejects gaps, forks, altered fields and records forged with anchor-account access even when the source host and PostgreSQL are lost. Provider issuance/revocation acknowledgements and receipts are verified independently of the phase signature through the separate administration credential. The capability secret remains only in the child, but another host's recovery root can use the signed mirrored selector and non-secret capability identity with that separately held provider-administration credential. On the normal path, exact object read-back records `result_recorded`; after owning chain DB CAS plus D-49 scope-projection checkpoint read-back/mirror CAS, root revokes/expires the exact operation capability, stores its independently verified provider acknowledgement, and CASes matching DB/off-host records to terminal `completed`. Every D-49 checkpoint-record operation—ordinary or D-51 cutover—is the non-recursive operation-kind exception: its own exact conditional checkpoint PUT/read-back plus mirror DB CAS is its complete owning acknowledgement and does not trigger or require a successor checkpoint. No other operation kind may claim this exception. Because the delivered capability names only the exact conditional immutable key, a duplicate accepted request cannot create another key; failed revocation or missing acknowledgement prevents completion. `completed` and emergency `drained` are the only terminal predicates. The fail-stop guardian owns both the child and shared advisory-lock session. On any lock-session loss before `completed`—whether the source host remains reachable or not—the guardian cancels/terminates the local child but may not issue a drain acknowledgement from local process/socket state or a negative HEAD. Root records `fence_requested`, revokes/fences the exact operation capability, stores the provider's authoritative acknowledgement/receipt checksum and barrier timestamp as `fence_acknowledged`, waits beyond `maxAcceptedRequestLifetime` measured from that acknowledgement, then stores/read-backs the first timestamped strong-consistent LIST/HEAD/inventory as `sweep_one_recorded` and the independently repeated second inventory as `sweep_two_recorded`; each is its own signed off-host phase and DB mirror CAS. Only matching `sweep_two_recorded` bytes may parent a separate terminal `drained` phase. A crash after the second sweep but before `drained` resumes from its exact read-back phase rather than repeating or eliding it. Any late bytes, including a commit after child exit and first negative inventory, are verified and forward-reconciled first. Unavailable provider/control namespace, missing selector/issuance or revocation receipt/lifetime, uncertain revocation, invalid phase signature/parent, weak consistency, inventory mismatch, requested/effective capability-policy mismatch, divergent mirror or lost lifecycle revision blocks global/core/lead release. A share-only nonterminal operation is quarantinable only if recovery re-verifies its actual effective capability policy through the provider and proves it cannot address global/core/lead; absent or stale provider evidence blocks release rather than merely shares. Terminal fence rows and phase records remain through the owning chain/artifact horizon; restore uses each scope prefix to recreate state newer than D-50. An authenticated, complete, single-descendant nonterminal prefix is resumable recovery input; incomplete/forged/forked/divergent phases fail closed for global/core/lead and are quarantinable for share only after D-42 isolation proof. The launch gate requires conditional create, strong read-after-write/list consistency, bounded accepted-request lifetime, deterministic capability discovery, provider-authenticated actual permission evidence, auditable per-operation revocation, and enforceable credential/key-prefix separation. Host-epoch credentials may mint only these recorded narrow operations; the finalizer uses two separately fenced global capabilities for its two pre-signed keys.

## What we deliberately do NOT do

- L-shaped, U-shaped, island, free-position, or 3D configuration; those remain Stage 5 or later.
- Customer accounts, saved-project dashboards, staff roles, CRM synchronization, email notifications, MAX bot delivery, WhatsApp outbound messages, or online payment.
- A general-purpose workflow engine, message broker, Redis, hosted queue, or microservice.
- A free-text customer comment or file upload.
- Contact data in local storage, share records, Telegram, browser URLs, analytics, or application logs.
- Automatic acceptance of a changed price or silent deletion/replacement of an unavailable module.
- Public share links in the initial Stage 4A lead release; Stage 4B is implemented and released only after the lead/retention path is accepted.

## User-visible flow

1. The visitor configures a straight kitchen as in Stage 3. Undo and redo are visible tap controls, and the current configuration is saved locally after changes.
2. On a later visit or refresh, the page offers to restore the local draft and immediately checks it against the server.
3. If publication data changed, the visitor sees what changed. An old and new exact total are shown when both are calculable. Unavailable choices remain in place with clear remove/replace actions.
4. The visitor resolves blocking issues and explicitly accepts the current price.
5. “Send request” opens a final review sheet with configuration, dimensions, warnings, specification, included/excluded items, price, and the minimal contact form.
6. The visitor opens the privacy notice and separate consent document, selects one preferred messenger, fills the required values, and checks the standalone consent control.
7. On success, the form becomes a stable success screen with one reference number. Refreshing or retrying the completed request returns the same reference.
8. The owner sees the reference in the protected inbox, opens the encrypted contact details and immutable configuration snapshot, changes the small workflow status, and can retry a failed Telegram notification.

## Architecture and ownership

### Shared contracts

`packages/contracts/src/drafts.ts` owns the versioned no-contact local draft and migrations' input/output schemas. `packages/contracts/src/leads.ts` owns reconciliation, legal-policy, public submission, success, owner list/detail/status, notification-retry, and later shared-draft DTOs. Contract exports continue only through `packages/contracts/src/index.ts`.

The public submission schema is strict and rejects browser totals, material rates, specification lines, arbitrary consent text, unknown fields, and overlong input. Contact normalization is deterministic and covered by contract tests; display formatting remains a UI concern.

### Backend modules

- `backend/src/modules/configuration` gains a reconciliation use case plus an exported pure snapshot calculator that can quote a caller-supplied immutable publication without database access or exposed pricing internals. Its existing HTTP service remains for ordinary configurator requests and is never used inside lead commit.
- New `backend/src/modules/leads` owns legal-policy lookup, contact normalization/encryption, idempotent submission, immutable lead snapshots, owner inbox/detail/status operations, retention cleanup, and the notification outbox.
- The leads application defines `LeadSubmissionUnitOfWork`; a composition-root infrastructure adapter is the sole cross-context transaction coordinator. It opens one Prisma transaction, uses transaction-bound repositories to lock/load intent, catalog, pricing, policy/transition/protection and persist the result, and calls only the configuration module's public pure calculator. Neither `Prisma.TransactionClient` nor configuration/catalog/pricing infrastructure types cross an application boundary.
- `backend/src/modules/leads/transport` exposes public legal/submission endpoints and owner-only inbox operations. Routes only translate HTTP and stable application errors.
- Telegram is a provider adapter behind a `LeadNotificationSender` port. The encrypted off-server deletion marker is behind a separate `ErasureRegister` port. Cron/erasure orchestration calls leads application services; transport/provider adapters do not own lead rules.
- `backend/src/app.ts` mounts `/api/leads` and `/api/owner/leads` and applies the separate public-lead write-security middleware.

Cross-context calls go through `configuration/index.ts` and explicit ports. The leads module must not import configuration, catalog, pricing, or auth infrastructure internals; the composition-root unit-of-work adapter is an explicit transaction boundary rather than a hidden application-to-infrastructure import.

### Public website

The Stage 3 configurator state is extracted from the single Astro script into small feature-owned modules under `website/src/features/configurator` before adding history, persistence, reconciliation, and submission. This is a responsibility split, not a new framework or dependency.

The UI keeps the existing mobile visual language and 44 × 44 px tap targets. The final review is a focused bottom sheet or full-height phone panel. Loading, offline, invalid draft, stale publication, unavailable item, changed price, consent error, rate limit, submission retry, and stable success states are all explicit.

### Owner cabinet

`webapp/src/features/leads` owns the authenticated API adapter, session-scoped TanStack Query keys/mutations, inbox, ephemeral detail selection, status mutation, and notification retry. The fixed `/app/leads` route composes both list and selected detail without placing a reference in the router URL/history; the main navigation exposes “Заявки” only to the authenticated owner.

The inbox uses cursor pagination and defaults to newest first. List responses contain no decrypted contact fields but are still sensitive. Every lead list/detail/mutation response is `private, no-store`; the entire lead query namespace is nested under `sessionQueryKeys`, cannot be persisted, uses `gcTime: 0`, and is cancelled/removed on unmount and every authenticated-session teardown. Detail selection exists only in feature memory, is cleared before history navigation, and is rebuilt only after a fresh authorized list interaction.

## Persistence model

The exact Prisma names may be adjusted to match generated-client conventions, but the responsibilities are fixed. Existing `CatalogPublication` also gains immutable nullable `publicVerifiedAt`; only the activation verifier or one-time evidence-backed bootstrap command may set it, and reconciliation never exposes a row where it is null.

Existing `AuthSession` additionally gains nullable `recoverySmokeOperationId`. Normal auth always writes null. During D-45 only the ephemeral smoke role may write its exact root-issued operation ID, and row-level policy plus a unique operation/session constraint limits that role's session DML to the owner and that one operation; revoking the credential before `clean` removes the exception.

- `LeadPolicyVersion`: immutable canonical UTF-8 privacy-notice and consent text, server-computed per-document SHA-256 checksums and full `policyBundleChecksum`, operator identity fields, retention days, recovery-window days, `telegram_enabled|telegram_disabled` delivery mode, nullable opaque delivery-config version plus keyed destination fingerprint, candidate creation timestamp, and optional activation timestamp. An inactive candidate is intentional durable state, not an orphan.
- `LeadPolicyCandidateWithdrawal`: immutable one-to-one record for a never-activated policy version, with unique operation ID/request digest, expected policy/transition revisions, non-sensitive reason, actor, stored result, D-46 sequence/hash acknowledgement, and timestamp. It cannot reference a current/former active version or transition candidate; config-pin release and success require the acknowledged epoch, and exact replay returns the stored result.

## Concurrency and failure behavior

- A stable minimal envelope is parsed first. The server computes domain-separated candidate digests under every unretired idempotency key and queries only `LeadSubmissionKey` before current full-schema, active-publication, or active-policy validation. Zero/one/multiple match behavior follows D-13. `open` may be claimed only before `openExpiresAt`; `submitting` returns pending unless the exact live claim continues; `committed` returns the original reference after prices/legal documents or receipt expiry; `tombstoned` returns `410 SUBMISSION_ERASED`; `abandoned_tombstone` rejects the old key while authorizing the browser to create a fresh one. Missing registry is never created by submission. An incoming schema or canonical payload fingerprint that disagrees with the authoritative registry returns `409`; receipt lookup is only a convenience view.
- Creating an intent locks or uniquely creates `LeadSubmissionKey`. Submission uses a short claim transaction that moves eligible `open → submitting`; D-8 then runs one calculation/lead transaction through the D-39 order, rechecks the claim, loads the immutable snapshot, calculates/validates, writes lead/notification, and commits the registry. Safe validation failure releases to unexpired `open`; if `openExpiresAt` passed it schedules D-48 and remains nonterminal until marker acknowledgement rather than writing `abandoned_tombstone` locally. A crash leaves a lease reconciled before retry. Policy activation/backup adoption follow D-39/WORM rules; external calls use the pre-call/zero-lock/post-call protocol.
- Concurrent intent, claim, submission, open-expiry, stale-claim recovery, receipt-expiry, and abandon requests serialize on one registry row. A paused claim before D-8 can expire only through D-48's atomic CAS from the exact expired `submitting` claim directly to `abandonment_pending`; it never becomes `open`. Once D-8 holds the row lock, cleanup waits. The same transaction verifies no committed lead/fingerprint and creates/reuses the abandonment attempt, so commit and cleanup have one winner with no submit window between them. Removal of a short-lived intent cannot free the key. If expiry/abandon wins while open or stale-submitting, late submit fails against the pending fence and then `abandoned_tombstone`; if commit wins, abandon cannot rotate the key and eventually returns the committed result. Before erasure, a different payload with that key is a visible conflict; erasure changes the registry to bounded `tombstoned`. Both tombstone kinds have explicit cleanup horizons, after which their old keys fall outside the documented replay guarantee and no longer pin versions.
- Publication changes before the submission transaction cause a reconciliation conflict and no write. Publication changes after the transaction lock wait until the accepted lead is committed. An active policy revision/checksum change causes `409 POLICY_CHANGED`, no lead write, policy refetch, visible document-change state, consent reset, and fresh acceptance.
- Notification dispatch-start, completion, and reschedule are compare-and-set operations on `id + leaseToken + expected status`; the provider timeout is shorter than the lease, a stale worker completion is a no-op, and attempts are capped before terminal failure. Lease recovery distinguishes safe pre-dispatch `leased` from ambiguous `dispatch_started`. An authoritative Telegram `ok=false` retryable result such as throttling reschedules the same row. Any ambiguous transport/result failure becomes `delivery_uncertain` without automatic resend. Fake-provider tests cover crashes before dispatch marking, after marking, after provider acceptance, and stale completion/manual resend.
- If the final HTTP response is lost after commit, receipt lookup or a retry with the same submission key returns the original success reference.
- Submission decoders/normalizers are versioned alongside contact and fingerprint codecs. A version cannot retire while any submission-key row, live lead, marker/control record, durable external attempt pin, or recoverable scoped artifact may route to it; D-34's writer fence prevents a new old-version row after the retirement scan, and restore verification exercises every unretired version.
- Retention/manual erasure is idempotent and safe to repeat. Request/job-operation lookup computes candidates under every unretired digest key. An existing manual pending request returns `202`; an existing bounded receipt returns success even if the lead is absent. Both manual and expiry first commit the `erasure_pending` fence and notification quiescence, then write/reuse the keyed external marker, detach control inventory, change the authoritative submission-key registry row to bounded `tombstoned` (plus manual receipt), delete the row-level graph, and retain no quote snapshot. Restore sanitation replays every marker: it deletes a resurrected lead when present and recreates/updates the unexpired registry tombstone even when the selected lead artifact predates the lead.
- Deletion ordering is durable fence/quiescence, encrypted acknowledged external marker, then one database transaction for attempt detach, receipt/tombstone, and lead/request deletion. A fence or marker without a live row is safe and retryable; deletion without durable marker is never complete. Scheduled expiry uses the same marker path specifically to preserve bounded idempotency semantics across a pre-lead backup, while its immutable expiry remains an additional restore check.

## Risks and mitigations

- **Legal text or retention is not approved:** lead capture stays disabled; configuration and local drafts still work without collecting personal data.
- **A stale draft contains removed catalog choices:** preserve and explain them, block exact submission, and require explicit replace/remove instead of silently mutating the kitchen.
- **Duplicate taps or ambiguous network completion:** durable idempotency returns the original committed reference.
- **Telegram outage, destination change, retention, or throttling:** the database is authoritative; every row is pinned to an immutable destination-config version, transitions drain/cancel rather than reroute, authoritative failures retry durably, and ambiguous outcomes stop in `delivery_uncertain`. The opaque reference is sent only after approval and cannot be promised recall.
- **Database/backup disclosure or bucket tampering:** application-level contact encryption, root-only local files, off-server public-key encryption, signed manifests/receipts/heads anchored outside the mutable data namespace, minimal fields, no payload logs, and bounded retention reduce exposure and reject forged recovery bundles.
- **Encryption, signing, or compatibility material loss:** the lead gate requires `age`-encrypted offline custody of every versioned application/signing keyring, retained compatible release artifacts/codecs, trusted recovery-head custody, an inventory scan of actual recovered data/markers, and fail-closed restore tests before collection or activation.
- **Restoring an old backup resurrects expired or manually erased leads:** restore accepts only an unexpired generation in the highest trusted signed head, then blocks traffic until overdue full-graph cleanup and the continuous external erasure head are applied; retirement denies restore before all physical copies/versions are purged.
- **Spam against a public form:** strict validation, honeypot, body/rate limits, idempotency, and owner-visible status launch first; CAPTCHA remains an evidence-driven fallback.
- **Single Astro file becomes unmaintainable:** extract the existing behavior into owned modules before layering persistence and forms, with Stage 3 E2E protecting behavior.
- **Scope growth or failure from sharing:** Stage 4B has a separate feature flag, schema, protection/readiness state, version inventory, tests, and release gate; share-only corruption disables share endpoints and cannot block a validated Stage 4A lead path.

## Rollback and compatibility

- Migrations are additive; existing Stage 3 routes and active catalog publications continue to work.
- `LEAD_CAPTURE_ENABLED=false` hides/disables final submission while leaving the configurator, local drafts, owner access to already stored leads, cleanup, notification maintenance, and backups intact.
- The Telegram worker can be paused operationally without disabling lead storage or the owner inbox; that pause preserves enabled-mode pending rows. A policy-level disabled mode instead creates terminal suppressed rows that can never be unsuppressed or backfilled.
- Before the first lead, the immediately previous Stage 3 application can run after migration because new tables/nullable fields are not required by Stage 3 code. After the first lead, deployment metadata and runbooks prohibit rollback below the latest validated Stage 4 maintenance-capable release.
- Personal data is never restored by an application rollback; database restore follows the encrypted recovery and overdue-cleanup gate.
